How to Get a YouTube Transcript (and Download It as TXT, SRT or VTT)

October 6, 2026 · Updated October 18, 2026 · 4 min read

TL;DR

Any YouTube video with captions has a transcript. You can read it under the video with Show transcript, or paste the link into the Transcript Download tool to copy it or save it as .txt, .srt or .vtt. If the video has no captions, there is nothing to fetch. Auto-generated captions contain mistakes, so read them before you quote them.

A transcript is the text of what is said in a video, with the time of each line. It is useful for taking notes, quoting accurately, writing a blog post from your own video, checking what a competitor covered, and making a video easier to read for people who cannot or prefer not to listen.

Three ways to get a YouTube transcript

1. Use YouTube's own transcript panel

YouTube's transcript help page says you can see the full transcript of any video that has captions. In the video description, click Show transcript. The text scrolls as you watch, and clicking a line jumps to that moment. This is the best choice when you only need to read along.

2. Use the Transcript Download tool

If you want the text as a file or as a clean block to paste somewhere, use the Transcript Download tool.

  1. Copy the video link.
  2. Paste it into the tool and press Get transcript.
  3. If the video has captions in more than one language, pick one.
  4. Tick Timestamps in text if you want the time before each line.
  5. Press Copy text, or download .txt, .srt or .vtt.

It reads the public captions that YouTube already shows to viewers, whether the creator uploaded them or YouTube generated them. It does not download video or audio.

3. Get your own captions from YouTube Studio

If it is your video, you can edit and download your own captions from the Subtitles area of YouTube Studio. This is the right place to fix mistakes, because fixing them there fixes the captions for every viewer.

How accurate is it?

The text is only as good as the captions. YouTube's automatic captioning help says automatic captions are made by machine learning, quality varies, and they "might misrepresent the spoken content" because of mispronunciations, accents, dialects or background noise. YouTube tells creators to review them and fix what is wrong.

So before you quote someone, check the sentence against the video. Names, numbers and technical terms are the most likely to be wrong.

Why a video might have no transcript

YouTube lists several reasons automatic captions may not exist: they are not ready yet because the audio is complex, the language is not supported, the video is too long, the sound quality is poor, there is a long silence at the start, or multiple speakers talk over each other. The creator can also turn captions off. If a video has no captions, the tool says so instead of making something up.

Sometimes YouTube blocks the lookup. The tool retries automatically, but if it still fails, try again a bit later or use the transcript panel on YouTube.

Using transcripts the right way

A transcript of someone else's video is still their work. Reading it for research is fine. Republishing it as your own article is not. If you quote, quote a short part and say where it is from. For your own videos, a transcript is a good starting point for a blog post, show notes or chapters, but rewrite it so it reads well in writing.

FAQ

How do I get a transcript of a YouTube video?

Open the video, expand the description and click Show transcript. Or paste the link into the Transcript Download tool and copy the text or save a .txt, .srt or .vtt file.

Why does a YouTube video have no transcript?

Usually because it has no captions. Auto-captions may be missing if the language is unsupported, the audio is poor or the video is very long, or the creator turned them off.

Are YouTube auto-generated captions accurate?

Not always. YouTube says they can misrepresent what was said because of accents, noise and similar issues. Check important parts.

Does this download the video?

No. It only reads the captions. This toolkit does not include video or audio downloaders.

Can I get a transcript in another language?

Only if the video has captions in that language. The tool shows the languages that exist for that video.
