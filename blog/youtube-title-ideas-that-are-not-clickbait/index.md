A title has two jobs. It has to match what a viewer is searching for, and it has to make the right person want to click. The [Title and Tag Ideas tool](https://devanasolver.github.io/youtube-creator-toolkit/#titles) helps with the first draft. This guide explains how to pick a good one and fix a weak one.

## What YouTube says about search

YouTube's [search help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16090438) says ranking looks at relevance, engagement and quality. For relevance it looks at how well the title, tags, description and the video content match the search. So the title matters, but it is only one signal. Nobody outside YouTube can tell you the exact weight of each part, and anyone who claims to is guessing.

## How to write a YouTube title from a topic

1. Start with the plain topic in words a viewer would type. "Indoor gardening for beginners" is better than a clever phrase nobody searches.
2. Pick an angle. Is this a how-to, a list, a mistake, a comparison, a story or a result? The angle is the reason to click.
3. Put the main phrase early. Long titles get cut off in search results and on phones, so the words at the front do the most work.
4. Keep it under 100 characters. That is YouTube's hard limit for a title, per its [video settings page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/57404). Staying near 60 is a safer habit so little gets cut.
5. Check the promise. Read the title, then read your first 30 seconds. If the video does not deliver what the title says, change the title.

## Ten title angles that work

The tool fills these patterns with your topic:

- **The hidden part:** "[Topic]: What Nobody Tells You"
- **The step by step:** "How to [Topic] (Step by Step)"
- **The test:** "I Tried [Topic] for 30 Days - Honest Results"
- **The time box:** "[Topic] Explained in Under 10 Minutes"
- **The myth bust:** "The Truth About [Topic]"
- **The mistakes list:** "7 [Topic] Mistakes Beginners Always Make"
- **The fix:** "Why Your [Topic] Isn't Working (and How to Fix It)"
- **The starter:** "[Topic] for Beginners: Start Here"
- **The pause:** "Before You Start [Topic], Watch This"
- **The guide:** "[Topic] in 2026: The Complete Guide"

Only use a pattern if it is true. "I Tried it for 30 Days" is only a title if you did.

## Clickbait is a real risk, not just a style problem

YouTube's [spam and deceptive practices policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973) lists malicious clickbait: titles, thumbnails or descriptions that trick people into clicking on a video that does not deliver what it promised. YouTube's own [click-through rate help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7628154) also says clickbait videos tend to have low average view duration, so they are less likely to be recommended. A title that overpromises can win the click and lose the video.

## Limits of a title generator

- It uses templates. It does not know your video, your audience or your niche, so some results will sound off. Delete those.
- It cannot predict views, ranking or click-through rate.
- It does not check what is already ranking. For that, use [keyword suggestions](https://devanasolver.github.io/youtube-creator-toolkit/blog/youtube-keyword-research-with-autocomplete/) and look at the search results yourself.

## FAQ

### How long should a YouTube title be?
YouTube allows up to 100 characters. Many creators aim for roughly 60 or fewer so the whole title shows on phones and in search results. That 60 figure is a common habit, not a YouTube rule.

### Do keywords in the title help?
Yes, relevance is one thing YouTube says it looks at, and the title is one of the places it checks. Use the words a viewer would actually type, and write it for a person first.

### Are numbers in titles better?
Not always. A number can make a promise concrete, such as "7 mistakes". It is a choice, not a requirement.

### Can a title generator guarantee more views?
No. No tool can. A title helps people understand the video and decide to click. Views depend on the video, the audience and what YouTube chooses to recommend.

### Should I change the title of an old video?
You can, but change one thing at a time so you can see the effect. YouTube also has a Test and Compare feature in Studio that can test up to three titles and thumbnails on eligible videos.
